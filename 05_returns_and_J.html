Read the tape bottom-up

Each new reward changes every earlier Gt — that's why G is computed backwards from the end: Gt = rt + γ·Gt+1. Drag γ and watch the early rows shrink.

Pink rows = the dice

You chose one way and slid another. That's T(s′|s,a) at work. "Replay" repeats your exact moves with fresh dice: same policy, different τ, different G.

One game is a terrible estimate

Look at the spread of single returns. The running mean (bottom chart) wobbles at first, then settles: its error shrinks like 1/√N. That's why the notebook plays 32 envs at once.

Training = climbing this

Gradient ascent wants to walk uphill. But in real RL we never get this map — it took thousands of games to draw. Press "sample again": the height of a single point is already noisy.

So what does the policy gradient do?

It estimates the slope at your point from the same few games you just played — no map, no physics. Part 3 is how.

click any square to stand there

One row of T

You stand on the dark square and choose an action. The arrows show T(s′ | s, a): the probability of landing on each square.

The agent only ever samples

It never sees these numbers. Each step the world rolls the dice once. Roll 100 times: the landing counts approach the probabilities.

Edges and walls

Slip into the wall or off the board and you stay put — so "stay here" can get probability too. Every row still adds up to 1. Slide slip to 0: deterministic, one arrow with probability 1.
