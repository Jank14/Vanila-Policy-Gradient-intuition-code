TARA · RL week 1 · interactive 15 · greedy or sample?

Why a policy should roll its dice

One state, two actions. Hidden from the agent: LEFT pays 10, RIGHT pays 3. It starts slightly preferring RIGHT. Both agents learn the same way: after each try, the action they took gets pushed up by its reward. The only difference is how they choose.

Greedy: always the argmax

It only ever learns about the action it picks. RIGHT keeps paying 3, so RIGHT keeps getting pushed up. It never finds out that LEFT pays 10.

Sample: roll the dice

LEFT still comes up about 45% of the time. Each LEFT pays 10: a bigger push than RIGHT's 3. Within a few tries LEFT wins. Exploration came for free.

The odds, by hand

Greedy tries LEFT with probability 0: it is stuck after try 1. A sampler that never updated would miss LEFT 10 times in a row with probability 0.5510 ≈ 0.25%. Learning only makes LEFT more likely once it has paid 10.

The trade-off

Sampling sometimes picks the worse action: that is the price of exploring. Greedy never pays it, and never improves either.
